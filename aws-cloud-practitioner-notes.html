Exam Overview

1. About the AWS Certified Cloud Practitioner Exam

  • Exam code: CLF-C02. Format: 65 questions (50 scored + 15 unscored, mixed in randomly and indistinguishable), multiple choice and multiple response.
  • Duration: 90 minutes. Cost: $100 USD. Delivery: Pearson VUE testing center or online proctored.
  • Scoring: Scaled score from 100-1000; passing score is 700. There is no penalty for wrong answers, so always answer every question.
  • Prerequisites: None required, though AWS recommends 6 months of exposure to the AWS Cloud in any role (technical, managerial, sales, purchasing, or financial).
  • Validity: The certification is valid for 3 years, after which it must be renewed by re-taking the exam or a higher-level exam.

Part A — Domain 1: Cloud Concepts (24%)

4. What is Cloud Computing?

Domain 1

Cloud computing is the on-demand delivery of IT resources (compute power, storage, databases, networking) over the internet, with pay-as-you-go pricing – instead of buying, owning, and maintaining physical data centers and servers.

  • Resources are provisioned in minutes via self-service, rather than weeks/months of procurement and setup for physical hardware.
  • You pay only for what you use, when you use it, with no long-term contracts required for most services.

5. Deployment Models

Domain 1
  • Cloud (All-in) – the application runs entirely in the cloud; all components/infrastructure are hosted on AWS.
  • Hybrid – cloud resources are connected to on-premises infrastructure, allowing gradual migration or integration (e.g. via AWS Direct Connect or AWS Outposts).
  • On-Premises (Private Cloud) – resources are deployed using virtualization and resource management tools to increase resource utilization within an organization's own data center.

6. Six Advantages of Cloud Computing

Domain 1Frequently Tested

These six advantages, as officially defined by AWS, are a very commonly tested list – know all six by name.

  • 1. Trade capital expense for variable expense – pay only for compute resources you consume, instead of investing heavily in data centers upfront.
  • 2. Benefit from massive economies of scale – AWS's large aggregate usage across all customers achieves lower pay-as-you-go prices than any single company could get on its own.
  • 3. Stop guessing capacity – scale up or down as needed, eliminating the guesswork of pre-provisioning for peak load.
  • 4. Increase speed and agility – new IT resources are available in minutes, dramatically increasing developer experimentation and innovation speed.
  • 5. Stop spending money running and maintaining data centers – focus on projects that differentiate the business, not on racking and stacking servers.
  • 6. Go global in minutes – deploy an application in multiple AWS Regions around the world with just a few clicks, providing lower latency to users globally.

7. Cloud Service Models (IaaS, PaaS, SaaS)

Domain 1
  • IaaS (Infrastructure as a Service) – provides the basic building blocks: networking, compute, storage. Maximum flexibility and control. Example: Amazon EC2.
  • PaaS (Platform as a Service) – removes the need to manage underlying infrastructure, letting you focus on deploying and managing applications. Example: AWS Elastic Beanstalk.
  • SaaS (Software as a Service) – a complete, ready-to-use product run and managed by the service provider; you just use it. Example: Gmail, or Amazon QuickSight for the end user.
  • As you move from IaaS → PaaS → SaaS, you manage less infrastructure yourself, but also get less low-level control.

8. Total Cost of Ownership (TCO)

Domain 1

TCO is the estimated financial benefit of moving to AWS, comparing the cost of running an application in a customer's own data center versus on AWS, accounting for both direct and indirect costs.

  • On-premises TCO includes hidden/indirect costs often overlooked: power, cooling, physical security, real estate, IT labor to maintain hardware.
  • AWS Pricing Calculator – a free tool to estimate the cost of AWS services for a planned architecture.
  • AWS TCO Calculator (part of Migration Evaluator) – compares the cost of an existing on-premises environment against running the equivalent workload on AWS.
  • CapEx vs OpEx: On-premises data centers are largely Capital Expenditure (large upfront investment); AWS Cloud is Operational Expenditure (pay-as-you-go, variable cost).

9. AWS Well-Architected Framework Overview

Domain 1Frequently Tested

A framework of best practices to help evaluate and design cloud architectures, structured around six equally-important "pillars". Each pillar is tested individually on the exam, so know all six names and their core focus.

  • Operational Excellence, Security, Reliability, Performance Efficiency, Cost Optimization, Sustainability.
  • Available as a free, self-service AWS Well-Architected Tool in the AWS Console, which reviews your architecture against these pillars and gives improvement recommendations.

10. Pillar 1: Operational Excellence

Domain 1

Focuses on running and monitoring systems to deliver business value, and continually improving processes and procedures.

  • Perform operations as code (e.g. Infrastructure as Code); make frequent, small, reversible changes; anticipate failure; learn from operational failures.

11. Pillar 2: Security

Domain 1

Focuses on protecting information, systems, and assets while delivering business value through risk assessments and mitigation strategies.

  • Implement a strong identity foundation; enable traceability; apply security at all layers; automate security best practices; protect data in transit and at rest.

12. Pillar 3: Reliability

Domain 1

Focuses on the ability of a system to recover from failures, dynamically acquire computing resources to meet demand, and mitigate disruptions.

  • Automatically recover from failure; test recovery procedures; scale horizontally to increase aggregate system availability; stop guessing capacity.

13. Pillar 4: Performance Efficiency

Domain 1

Focuses on using computing resources efficiently to meet requirements, and maintaining that efficiency as demand changes and technologies evolve.

  • Democratize advanced technologies (use managed services instead of building expertise from scratch); go global in minutes; use serverless architectures; experiment more often.

14. Pillar 5: Cost Optimization

Domain 1

Focuses on avoiding unnecessary costs, understanding spending over time, and scaling to meet business needs without overspending.

  • Adopt a consumption model (pay only for what you use); measure overall efficiency; stop spending on undifferentiated heavy lifting (data center operations); analyze and attribute expenditure.

15. Pillar 6: Sustainability

Domain 1

The newest pillar (added 2021), focused on minimizing the environmental impact of running cloud workloads.

  • Understand your impact; establish sustainability goals; maximize utilization; use managed services (which are shared across many customers, improving overall efficiency); reduce the downstream impact of your workloads.

Part B — AWS Global Infrastructure

16. Regions

Domain 3Frequently Tested

A Region is a physical, geographically isolated location in the world containing multiple, fully independent Availability Zones. As of the current exam version, AWS has over 30 Regions worldwide.

  • Each Region is completely independent of other Regions, providing fault tolerance, stability, and data sovereignty (data does not leave a Region unless you explicitly configure it to).
  • Not all AWS services are available in all Regions – newer/specialized services often launch in a limited set of Regions first.
  • Pricing varies by Region due to differing local costs (electricity, real estate, labor, taxes).

17. Availability Zones (AZs)

Domain 3Frequently Tested

An Availability Zone is one or more discrete data centers with redundant power, networking, and connectivity, housed in separate facilities. Every AWS Region has a minimum of 3 Availability Zones.

  • AZs within a Region are interconnected with high-bandwidth, low-latency networking, but are physically separated (miles apart) to isolate them from failures like fires, floods, or power outages in any one location.
  • Designing an application to use multiple AZs is a core best practice for high availability and fault tolerance.

18. Edge Locations & Points of Presence

Domain 3

Edge locations are sites that Amazon CloudFront (AWS's Content Delivery Network) uses to cache copies of content closer to end users for lower latency – there are far more Edge locations than Regions (hundreds worldwide).

  • Used by CloudFront and AWS Global Accelerator to serve cached content quickly, without every request needing to travel back to the origin Region.
  • A "Point of Presence" is a collective term for both Edge locations and Regional Edge Caches.

19. Choosing a Region

Domain 3Frequently Tested

The exam often presents a scenario and asks which factor should determine the Region choice. Four official factors:

  • Compliance – data governance and legal requirements may mandate data stay within specific geographic/legal boundaries.
  • Latency – choose a Region physically close to your users to minimize response time.
  • Pricing – costs for the same service can differ meaningfully between Regions.
  • Service availability – not every AWS service or feature is available in every Region.

20. Ways to Access/Interact with AWS

Domain 3
  • AWS Management Console – a web-based visual interface for managing AWS resources.
  • AWS Command Line Interface (CLI) – a unified tool to manage AWS services by typing commands in a terminal, ideal for scripting and automation.
  • AWS Software Development Kits (SDKs) – libraries for programming languages (Python, Java, JavaScript, etc.) that let applications call AWS services directly from code.
# Example AWS CLI command - list all S3 buckets
aws s3 ls

# Example AWS CLI command - list running EC2 instances
aws ec2 describe-instances --filters "Name=instance-state-name,Values=running"

21. Infrastructure as Code with AWS CloudFormation

Domain 3

AWS CloudFormation lets you define your entire AWS infrastructure (servers, databases, networking) as a text file (JSON or YAML template), which can then be version-controlled and deployed repeatably – instead of manually clicking through the Console every time.

  • A "stack" is the collection of AWS resources created and managed together from one template.
  • Enables consistent, repeatable, and automated infrastructure deployment – a core practice of the Operational Excellence pillar.
  • Free to use – you only pay for the underlying AWS resources the template creates.
# A minimal CloudFormation template (YAML) that creates one S3 bucket
Resources:
  MyBucket:
    Type: AWS::S3::Bucket
    Properties:
      BucketName: my-example-bucket-12345

Part C — Domain 2: Security and Compliance (30%)

22. The Shared Responsibility Model

Domain 2Most Tested Single Topic

This is arguably the single most frequently tested concept on the entire exam. It defines exactly which security tasks AWS handles versus which the customer must handle.

  • AWS is responsible for "Security OF the Cloud" – protecting the infrastructure that runs all AWS services: hardware, software, networking, and the physical facilities.
  • The customer is responsible for "Security IN the Cloud" – this varies by service, but generally includes: customer data, IAM configuration, operating system/network/firewall configuration (for EC2), platform and application management, encryption of data (client-side and server-side).
  • Rule of thumb: for managed services (like RDS, S3, DynamoDB), AWS manages more of the underlying stack; for unmanaged/IaaS services (like EC2), the customer manages more (e.g. patching the guest OS is always the customer's job on EC2).

23. AWS Compliance Programs & AWS Artifact

Domain 2

AWS undergoes independent, third-party audits against many global compliance standards, and makes the resulting reports available to customers as proof for their own compliance audits.

  • Examples of compliance programs AWS participates in: ISO 27001, SOC 1/2/3, PCI DSS, HIPAA, GDPR, FedRAMP.
  • AWS Artifact – a free, self-service portal in the Console to download AWS's compliance reports (e.g. SOC reports) and agreements (e.g. Business Associate Addendum for HIPAA), on demand, at no cost.

24. IAM: Users, Groups, Roles

Domain 2Frequently Tested

AWS Identity and Access Management (IAM) controls who can do what within an AWS account. IAM is a global service (not tied to any one Region) and is free to use.

  • IAM User – represents one person or application, with its own credentials (password and/or access keys).
  • IAM Group – a collection of IAM users; permissions attached to a Group apply to every user in it – the recommended way to manage permissions for many similar users at once.
  • IAM Role – a set of permissions that can be temporarily assumed by a user, application, or AWS service (e.g. an EC2 instance) – does not have permanent long-term credentials, unlike a User. Best practice for granting an AWS service (like EC2 or Lambda) permission to access other AWS services.

25. IAM Policies

Domain 2Frequently Tested

A Policy is a JSON document that explicitly defines permissions – it is attached to a User, Group, or Role to grant (or deny) access to specific AWS actions and resources.

  • Effect – "Allow" or "Deny". Action – the specific API operation(s), e.g. s3:GetObject. Resource – the specific AWS resource(s) the policy applies to, identified by its ARN.
  • By default, an IAM user/role has NO permissions (implicit deny) – permissions must be explicitly granted.
  • An explicit "Deny" in any applicable policy always overrides an "Allow" from another policy.
  • Principle of Least Privilege – grant only the minimum permissions required to perform a task, nothing more; a core, heavily-tested security best practice.

Example: this policy allows reading and uploading objects, but ONLY inside my-example-bucket, and grants no permission to delete objects or access any other bucket:

{
  "Version": "2012-10-17",
  "Statement": [
    {
      "Effect": "Allow",
      "Action": [
        "s3:GetObject",
        "s3:PutObject"
      ],
      "Resource": "arn:aws:s3:::my-example-bucket/*"
    }
  ]
}

26. MFA & Root Account Best Practices

Domain 2Frequently Tested

Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA) requires a second, independent proof of identity (a one-time code from a device) in addition to a password – a critical, heavily-tested security layer.

  • The Root user is created automatically when an AWS account is opened, has unrestricted access to everything, and cannot have its permissions restricted – it should almost never be used for day-to-day work.
  • Best practice: enable MFA on the root user immediately, then create an individual IAM user (or use IAM Identity Center) with appropriate permissions for daily administrative tasks, and lock the root credentials away securely.
  • MFA devices supported: virtual MFA apps (e.g. an authenticator app), hardware MFA key fobs, and security keys.

27. AWS Organizations & Service Control Policies (SCPs)

Domain 2

AWS Organizations lets you centrally manage and govern multiple AWS accounts as a single organization – e.g. separate accounts per department or environment (dev/test/prod).

  • Service Control Policies (SCPs) – set the maximum available permissions for accounts within an Organization; they do NOT grant permissions themselves, only restrict what IAM policies within that account can allow.
  • Also enables Consolidated Billing (Section 82) – a single, combined bill across all member accounts, plus volume pricing discounts calculated across the combined usage.

29. AWS Key Management Service (KMS)

Domain 2

A managed service to create and control encryption keys used to encrypt data across AWS services (S3, EBS, RDS, etc.), without needing to build your own key management infrastructure.

  • Integrates directly with most AWS storage/database services for transparent "encryption at rest".
  • Supports both AWS-managed keys (created automatically) and Customer-managed keys (full control over key policy and rotation).

30. AWS Shield

Domain 2

A managed service protecting against Distributed Denial of Service (DDoS) attacks.

  • AWS Shield Standard – automatically enabled at no additional cost for ALL AWS customers, protecting against the most common, frequently occurring network/transport layer DDoS attacks.
  • AWS Shield Advanced – a paid subscription offering enhanced protections for larger/more sophisticated attacks, 24/7 access to a DDoS Response Team, and cost protection for scaling charges incurred during an attack.

31. AWS WAF (Web Application Firewall)

Domain 2

Protects web applications from common web exploits by letting you define rules that filter/block malicious HTTP/HTTPS traffic (e.g. SQL injection, cross-site scripting), before it reaches your application.

  • Deployed in front of CloudFront, Application Load Balancer, or API Gateway.
  • Operates at Layer 7 (application layer), complementing Shield's network-layer protection.

32. Amazon GuardDuty

Domain 2

An intelligent threat detection service that continuously monitors for malicious activity and unauthorized behavior, using machine learning, anomaly detection, and threat intelligence feeds – analyzing account/network activity logs (like CloudTrail and VPC Flow Logs) automatically.

33. Amazon Inspector

Domain 2

An automated security assessment service that scans EC2 instances and container images for software vulnerabilities and unintended network exposure.

34. Amazon Macie

Domain 2

Uses machine learning to automatically discover, classify, and protect sensitive data (like personally identifiable information, PII) stored in Amazon S3.

35. AWS Security Hub

Domain 2

A central dashboard that aggregates, organizes, and prioritizes security findings/alerts from multiple AWS security services (GuardDuty, Inspector, Macie) and third-party tools into one consolidated view.

36. AWS Config

Domain 2

Records and tracks the configuration history of AWS resources over time, and can evaluate resource configurations against desired rules – used for configuration compliance auditing (e.g. "flag any S3 bucket that becomes publicly readable").

37. AWS CloudTrail

Domain 2Frequently Tested

Records every API call/action made within an AWS account (who did what, when, and from where) – essential for governance, compliance auditing, and security investigation.

  • Exam distinction: CloudTrail logs API activity/actions (the "who did what"); CloudWatch (Section 74) monitors performance metrics and logs application/system behavior (the "how is it performing"). This distinction is commonly tested.
  • Enabled by default for the last 90 days of account activity, viewable in the Console at no charge; can be configured to deliver logs to S3 for long-term retention.

38. Amazon Cognito

Domain 2

Provides authentication, authorization, and user management specifically for customer-facing web and mobile applications – distinct from IAM, which manages access for AWS account administrators/resources, not app end-users.

  • Supports sign-up/sign-in directly, plus federation with social identity providers (Google, Facebook) and enterprise identity providers (SAML, OIDC).

39. AWS Security Best Practices Summary

Domain 2Frequently Tested
  • Enable MFA on the root account and all IAM users, especially privileged ones.
  • Follow the Principle of Least Privilege for every IAM policy.
  • Use IAM Roles (not long-term access keys) to grant AWS services access to other AWS services.
  • Never share or hard-code root account credentials or access keys in application code.
  • Enable CloudTrail across all Regions for full auditability.
  • Encrypt sensitive data at rest (via KMS) and in transit (via TLS/SSL).

Part D — Compute Services

40. Amazon EC2 Basics

Domain 3Core Service

Amazon Elastic Compute Cloud (EC2) provides resizable virtual servers ("instances") in the cloud – the foundational IaaS compute service.

  • Launched from an AMI (Amazon Machine Image) – a template containing the OS and any pre-installed software.
  • The customer manages the guest OS, applications, and data (per the Shared Responsibility Model); AWS manages the underlying physical hardware and virtualization layer.
  • EBS (Elastic Block Store) volumes provide persistent block storage that survives instance stop/termination (unlike "instance store" volumes, which are physically attached and lost when the instance stops).

41. EC2 Instance Types

Domain 3

Instance types are grouped into families optimized for different workload characteristics – know the general family letter meanings, not exact specs.

  • General Purpose (e.g. T, M families) – balanced compute, memory, and networking; good default choice for web servers, small databases.
  • Compute Optimized (C family) – high-performance processors; good for batch processing, media transcoding, high-performance computing.
  • Memory Optimized (R, X families) – fast performance for memory-intensive workloads like large in-memory databases.
  • Storage Optimized (I, D families) – high, fast, local storage I/O for data warehousing, distributed file systems.
  • Accelerated Computing (P, G families) – use hardware GPU accelerators, for machine learning and graphics-intensive workloads.

42. EC2 Pricing Options

Domain 3Domain 4Frequently Tested

One of the most heavily tested topics on the exam – know which pricing model fits which use case.

  • On-Demand – pay by the second/hour with no long-term commitment; best for short-term, unpredictable, or spiky workloads, or for applications being developed/tested for the first time.
  • Reserved Instances (RI) – commit to a 1 or 3-year term for a significant discount (up to ~75%) versus On-Demand; best for steady-state, predictable usage.
  • Savings Plans – commit to a consistent amount of compute usage ($/hour) for 1 or 3 years, in exchange for lower prices, with more flexibility across instance families/Regions than Reserved Instances.
  • Spot Instances – bid on unused EC2 capacity for up to ~90% off On-Demand price; AWS can reclaim the instance with only a 2-minute warning, so best for fault-tolerant, flexible workloads (batch jobs, big data processing) – NEVER for critical/persistent workloads.
  • Dedicated Hosts – a physical server fully dedicated to one customer's use, often required for licensing that is tied to physical cores/sockets, or strict compliance needs.

43. Amazon EC2 Auto Scaling

Domain 3

Automatically adds or removes EC2 instances according to demand, based on defined conditions (e.g. CPU utilization), to maintain application availability and control cost by not over-provisioning.

  • Configured with a minimum, desired, and maximum number of instances.
  • Directly supports the Reliability pillar (Section 12) and Cost Optimization pillar (Section 14) of the Well-Architected Framework.

44. Elastic Load Balancing (ELB)

Domain 3

Automatically distributes incoming application traffic across multiple targets (EC2 instances, containers) in one or more Availability Zones, increasing fault tolerance.

  • Application Load Balancer (ALB) – operates at Layer 7 (HTTP/HTTPS), supports content-based routing.
  • Network Load Balancer (NLB) – operates at Layer 4 (TCP/UDP), for extreme performance and low latency.
  • Gateway Load Balancer (GWLB) – for deploying/scaling third-party virtual network appliances.

45. AWS Lambda

Domain 3Core Service

A serverless compute service that runs code in response to events, without provisioning or managing any servers at all.

  • Pricing – pay only for the compute time actually consumed (billed per millisecond), with no charge when code is not running – a very commonly tested pricing distinction versus EC2 (which bills for the whole instance running, whether used or idle).
  • Automatically scales by running more instances of the function in parallel as needed – no capacity planning required.
  • Ideal for short-running, event-driven tasks (e.g. resizing an image the moment it's uploaded to S3).

46. Containers: Amazon ECS, Amazon EKS & AWS Fargate

Domain 3
  • Amazon ECS (Elastic Container Service) – AWS's own, native container orchestration service for running and managing Docker containers.
  • Amazon EKS (Elastic Kubernetes Service) – a managed service for running the popular open-source Kubernetes container orchestration platform on AWS.
  • AWS Fargate – a serverless compute engine for containers, usable with both ECS and EKS, which removes the need to provision or manage the underlying EC2 instances that containers run on.

47. AWS Elastic Beanstalk

Domain 3

A PaaS offering: simply upload your application code, and Elastic Beanstalk automatically handles the deployment details – capacity provisioning, load balancing, auto scaling, and health monitoring – while still giving you access to the underlying resources if needed.

48. Amazon Lightsail

Domain 3

The simplest way to get started on AWS – bundles compute, storage, and networking into a single, low, predictable monthly price, aimed at simple web applications, blogs, or for those new to AWS who don't need the full flexibility of EC2.

49. AWS Outposts & Hybrid Compute

Domain 3

AWS Outposts extends AWS infrastructure, services, and tools to virtually any on-premises data center or co-location space – physical AWS-designed hardware racks are installed on-site, running the same APIs and tools as the AWS Cloud, for workloads needing extremely low latency to on-premises systems or local data processing requirements.

Part E — Storage Services

50. Amazon S3

Domain 3Core Service

Amazon Simple Storage Service (S3) is object storage built to store and retrieve any amount of data from anywhere – one of the most heavily tested services on the exam.

  • Data is stored as "objects" inside "buckets"; bucket names must be globally unique across all of AWS.
  • Durability: 99.999999999% ("11 nines") – designed so that if you store 10,000,000 objects, you can expect to lose one object roughly once every 10,000 years.
  • Availability: 99.99% for the S3 Standard storage class – note durability and availability are different, commonly confused figures.
  • Not a file system or a block storage device – accessed via a simple web API (HTTP GET/PUT), not mounted like a drive.

51. S3 Storage Classes

Domain 3Frequently Tested

Different classes trade off retrieval speed/availability against storage cost – know which class fits which access pattern.

S3 Standard-IA (Infrequent Access)Accessed less often, but needs rapid access when needed; lower storage cost, retrieval fee applies

52. S3 Versioning, Lifecycle Policies & Replication

Domain 3
  • Versioning – keeps every version of an object when overwritten or deleted, protecting against accidental deletion/overwrite and enabling recovery to a prior version. Once enabled, it cannot be fully disabled, only suspended.
  • Lifecycle Policies – automatically transition objects to cheaper storage classes, or delete them, after a defined number of days – e.g. move to Glacier after 90 days, delete after 365 days.
  • Cross-Region Replication (CRR) – automatically and asynchronously copies objects to a bucket in a different Region, for compliance, lower-latency access, or disaster recovery.

53. Amazon EBS (Elastic Block Store)

Domain 3

Provides persistent, high-performance block storage volumes that attach to a single EC2 instance – like a virtual hard drive.

  • Data persists independently from the life of the instance (unlike instance store volumes).
  • An EBS volume is tied to a specific Availability Zone – it can only attach to instances within that same AZ.
  • Supports point-in-time snapshots, stored in S3, which can be used to create new volumes or copy data across AZs/Regions.

54. Amazon EFS (Elastic File System)

Domain 3

A fully managed, scalable NFS (Network File System) that can be mounted concurrently by MANY EC2 instances at the same time, across multiple Availability Zones – the key distinction from EBS, which attaches to only one instance at a time.

55. AWS Storage Gateway

Domain 3

A hybrid cloud storage service connecting an on-premises environment to AWS storage – presents cloud storage using standard on-premises storage protocols (NFS, SMB, iSCSI), letting existing on-premises applications use S3 without any code changes.

56. AWS Snow Family

Domain 3

Physical devices for transferring very large amounts of data into or out of AWS, or for edge computing in disconnected/remote locations – used when network transfer would be too slow or costly.

  • AWS Snowcone – smallest device, for edge computing/data transfer in limited spaces.
  • AWS Snowball – a ruggedized physical device, shipped to your location, to transfer up to petabytes of data.
  • AWS Snowmobile – an entire shipping container on a truck, for exabyte-scale data transfers.

Part F — Database Services

57. Amazon RDS (Relational Database Service)

Domain 3Core Service

A managed service for relational (SQL) databases – AWS handles routine tasks like patching, backups, and hardware provisioning, so you focus on the data and queries.

  • Supports multiple database engines: MySQL, PostgreSQL, MariaDB, Oracle, SQL Server, and Amazon Aurora.
  • Multi-AZ deployment – automatically maintains a synchronous standby replica in a different AZ for high availability/failover, NOT for read scaling.
  • Read Replicas – asynchronous, read-only copies used to scale out READ-heavy workloads, can be in the same or different Regions – a commonly tested distinction from Multi-AZ.

58. Amazon Aurora

Domain 3

AWS's own cloud-native relational database engine, compatible with MySQL and PostgreSQL, offering up to 5x the throughput of standard MySQL and 3x that of standard PostgreSQL, at a fraction of the cost of commercial databases – runs as an option within RDS.

59. Amazon DynamoDB

Domain 3Core Service

A fully managed, serverless NoSQL (key-value and document) database, delivering consistent single-digit millisecond performance at any scale.

  • Exam distinction: RDS/Aurora = SQL/relational (structured tables with defined schemas, relationships); DynamoDB = NoSQL (flexible schema, key-value/document access patterns) – know when a scenario calls for each.
  • Automatically scales throughput and storage with no server management at all.

60. Amazon Redshift

Domain 3

A fully managed data warehouse service, used for large-scale analytics and business intelligence – optimized for complex queries across massive datasets (petabyte scale), NOT for everyday transactional application queries.

61. Amazon ElastiCache

Domain 3

A managed in-memory caching service (supporting Redis or Memcached engines), used to dramatically speed up application performance by retrieving frequently-accessed data from fast memory instead of a slower disk-based database.

62. AWS Database Migration Service (DMS)

Domain 3

Helps migrate databases into AWS quickly and securely – the source database remains fully operational during the migration, minimizing downtime for applications relying on it. Can also migrate between different database engines (e.g. Oracle to Aurora) using the related AWS Schema Conversion Tool.

Part G — Networking Services

63. Amazon VPC (Virtual Private Cloud)

Domain 3Core Service

A logically isolated, private virtual network within AWS where you launch resources, with complete control over IP address ranges, subnets, and routing – the foundation of AWS networking.

  • Each VPC is isolated from other VPCs by default, unless explicitly connected (e.g. via VPC Peering or Transit Gateway).
  • A default VPC is automatically created in every Region for every AWS account, ready to use immediately.

64. Subnets, Route Tables & Gateways

Domain 3Frequently Tested
  • Subnet – a range of IP addresses within a VPC, always located entirely within a single Availability Zone.
  • Public Subnet – has a route to an Internet Gateway, so resources within it can be reached from the internet.
  • Private Subnet – has NO route to an Internet Gateway, keeping resources (like a database) isolated from direct internet access.
  • Internet Gateway (IGW) – allows communication between resources in a VPC and the internet; one per VPC.
  • NAT Gateway – allows instances in a PRIVATE subnet to initiate outbound internet connections (e.g. for software updates) while still preventing the internet from initiating inbound connections to them.
  • Route Table – a set of rules determining where network traffic from a subnet is directed.

65. Amazon Route 53

Domain 3

A highly available and scalable Domain Name System (DNS) web service – translates human-readable domain names (like example.com) into IP addresses.

  • Can also register new domain names directly, and perform health checks to route traffic away from unhealthy endpoints.
  • The name "53" refers to the standard port number DNS uses.

66. Amazon CloudFront

Domain 3Frequently Tested

AWS's Content Delivery Network (CDN) – caches copies of content (web pages, videos, images) at Edge Locations (Section 18) around the world, close to end users, to reduce latency.

  • The origin (source of the original content) can be S3, an EC2 instance, an Elastic Load Balancer, or any custom HTTP server.
  • Also integrates with AWS Shield and AWS WAF for edge-level security protection.

67. AWS Direct Connect & VPN

Domain 3Frequently Tested
  • AWS Direct Connect – establishes a DEDICATED, private, physical network connection from an on-premises data center directly into AWS, bypassing the public internet entirely – provides more consistent network performance and can reduce bandwidth costs; takes time (weeks) to provision the physical link.
  • AWS Site-to-Site VPN – creates an encrypted connection between an on-premises network and a VPC OVER the public internet – much faster to set up than Direct Connect, but with less consistent performance since it traverses the public internet.
  • AWS Client VPN – allows individual users to securely connect to AWS resources and their on-premises network from any location using an OpenVPN-based client.

68. AWS Transit Gateway

Domain 3

Acts as a central hub that simplifies network connections between multiple VPCs and on-premises networks – instead of creating individual peering connections between every pair of VPCs (which grows complex quickly), each VPC connects once to the Transit Gateway.

Part H — App Integration, Analytics & Management

69. Amazon SNS (Simple Notification Service)

Domain 3Frequently Tested

A fully managed pub/sub (publish/subscribe) messaging service – a message published to a "topic" is instantly pushed out to every subscriber of that topic (email, SMS, Lambda, SQS queues, mobile push) at once.

  • Fan-out pattern: one message, many simultaneous recipients – the key exam distinction from SQS (below), which delivers to consumers pulling from a single queue, not broadcasting to many at once.

70. Amazon SQS (Simple Queue Service)

Domain 3Frequently Tested

A fully managed message queuing service that lets you decouple and scale distributed application components – a producer places messages onto a queue, and a consumer retrieves and processes them independently, at its own pace.

  • Standard Queue – nearly unlimited throughput, "best-effort" ordering, and at-least-once delivery (a message could rarely be delivered more than once).
  • FIFO Queue – preserves strict First-In-First-Out message order and exactly-once processing, with somewhat lower throughput than Standard.
  • SNS vs SQS (very commonly tested): SNS pushes a message out to MANY subscribers at once (fan-out); SQS holds messages in a queue for ONE (or a pool of) consumer(s) to pull and process at their own pace. They are very often used together: SNS fans a message out to multiple SQS queues.

71. Amazon API Gateway

Domain 3

A fully managed service to create, publish, secure, and monitor APIs at any scale – commonly used as the front door for serverless applications, routing incoming HTTP requests to AWS Lambda functions.

72. AWS Step Functions & Amazon EventBridge

Domain 3
  • AWS Step Functions – a visual workflow service to coordinate multiple AWS services (especially Lambda functions) into a defined sequence of steps, handling retries, error handling, and state management automatically.
  • Amazon EventBridge – a serverless event bus that connects applications together using data from your own apps, AWS services, and SaaS applications, routing events to targets based on defined rules.

73. Analytics Overview: Athena & QuickSight

Domain 3
  • Amazon Athena – an interactive, serverless query service to analyze data directly in S3 using standard SQL, with no infrastructure to manage and no data loading required.
  • Amazon QuickSight – a serverless business intelligence (BI) service to build interactive dashboards and visualizations from AWS data sources.

74. Amazon CloudWatch

Domain 3Frequently Tested

A monitoring and observability service that collects metrics, logs, and events from AWS resources and applications, providing dashboards, alarms, and automated actions.

  • CloudWatch Metrics – numeric data points over time (e.g. EC2 CPU utilization).
  • CloudWatch Alarms – watch a metric and trigger an action (e.g. send a notification via SNS, or trigger Auto Scaling) when a threshold is breached.
  • CloudWatch Logs – centralized storage and analysis for log files from applications and AWS services.
  • Exam distinction (repeated for emphasis): CloudWatch = performance/operational monitoring ("how is it running"); CloudTrail (Section 37) = API activity auditing ("who did what").

75. AWS Systems Manager

Domain 3

A unified operations hub to view operational data and automate operational tasks across AWS resources – e.g. patching EC2 instances at scale, running commands remotely, or securely storing configuration data/secrets (Parameter Store).

76. AWS Trusted Advisor

Domain 3Domain 4Frequently Tested

An automated service that inspects your AWS environment and provides real-time recommendations across five categories, following AWS best practices.

  • The five checks: Cost Optimization, Performance, Security, Fault Tolerance, and Service Limits.
  • Basic and Developer Support plans get access only to a limited set of core security and service-limit checks.
  • Business, Enterprise On-Ramp, and Enterprise Support plans get access to the FULL set of Trusted Advisor checks across all five categories, plus the ability to set up CloudWatch alarms based on check status.

Part I — Domain 4: Billing, Pricing, and Support (12%)

77. AWS Free Tier

Domain 4

Lets new (and some existing) customers use certain AWS services free, up to specified limits, to gain hands-on experience.

  • 12 Months Free – a set of offers valid for 12 months after account sign-up (e.g. 750 hours/month of a t2.micro/t3.micro EC2 instance, 5GB of S3 Standard storage).
  • Always Free – offers that never expire, available to all customers (e.g. Lambda's first 1 million requests per month).
  • Trials – short-term free trials starting from the date a specific service is first activated, regardless of account age.

78. Pricing Models Overview

Domain 4Frequently Tested

Three fundamental AWS pricing principles apply across virtually all services.

  • Pay for what you use – no upfront investment, no long-term commitments required (though committing can lower the price, e.g. Reserved Instances).
  • Pay less when you reserve/commit – Reserved Instances and Savings Plans lower cost in exchange for a usage commitment.
  • Pay less with volume, when you use more – e.g. S3 storage price per GB decreases as total stored volume increases (tiered pricing).
  • Data Transfer: transferring data INTO AWS is generally free; transferring data OUT to the internet is generally charged (and is often the largest, most overlooked cost in an AWS bill).

79. AWS Budgets

Lets you set custom cost and usage budgets that alert you (via email/SNS) when actual or forecasted spend exceeds (or is about to exceed) your defined threshold.

80. AWS Cost Explorer

81. AWS Cost and Usage Report (CUR)

90. Common Exam Traps & Tips

Exam Reference
  • If a question describes a scenario needing "security OF the cloud" vs "security IN the cloud", it is testing the Shared Responsibility Model (Section 22) – re-read carefully to identify which side is being asked about.
  • Questions mentioning "no upfront cost, pay only for what you use, no long-term commitment" almost always point to On-Demand pricing or a serverless service like Lambda.
  • Questions mentioning "steady, predictable, long-running workload, wants to save money" point to Reserved Instances or Savings Plans.
  • Questions mentioning "interruptible, fault-tolerant, flexible timing, wants cheapest possible price" point to Spot Instances.
  • If a question is about scaling READS from a database, think Read Replicas; if about HIGH AVAILABILITY/failover, think Multi-AZ.
  • If a question is about broadcasting to MANY subscribers, think SNS; if about ONE queue being processed by a worker, think SQS.
  • There is no penalty for guessing – never leave a question unanswered.
  • Eliminate obviously wrong answers first (often 1-2 options are clearly incorrect), then choose between the remaining plausible ones.

Part K — Assignments

Assignment 1 — Write an IAM Policy for a Scenario

Task: A company has a "read-only-auditor" role that must be able to LIST and READ objects in the bucket "company-reports" only, but must NEVER be able to delete or upload objects, and must have no access to any other bucket.

Solution 
{
  "Version": "2012-10-17",
  "Statement": [
    {
      "Effect": "Allow",
      "Action": [
        "s3:ListBucket",
        "s3:GetObject"
      ],
      "Resource": [
        "arn:aws:s3:::company-reports",
        "arn:aws:s3:::company-reports/*"
      ]
    }
  ]
}

Explanation: Only s3:ListBucket and s3:GetObject are allowed (read-only actions) – no PutObject or DeleteObject action is granted, so uploads/deletes are automatically denied (implicit deny). The Resource is restricted to exactly this one bucket (and objects within it), so no other bucket is accessible. This follows the Principle of Least Privilege (Section 25).

Assignment 2 — Choose the Right Pricing Model

Task: For each scenario below, name the best-fit EC2 pricing model and justify it in one line.

Solution 
(a) A batch video-rendering job that can pause and resume anytime, and just needs
    to finish sometime today at the lowest possible cost.
    -> SPOT INSTANCES: fault-tolerant, flexible timing, cheapest option; can handle
       a 2-minute interruption warning without issue.

(b) A company's core production database server that will run 24/7 for the next 3 years.
    -> RESERVED INSTANCES (3-year term) or SAVINGS PLANS: steady, predictable, long-term
       usage qualifies for the largest possible discount.

(c) A brand-new startup testing an MVP application with completely unknown, unpredictable
    traffic for the next few months.
    -> ON-DEMAND: no commitment needed while usage patterns are still unknown; can switch
       to Reserved/Savings Plans later once usage becomes predictable.

(d) A software vendor whose licensing is tied to physical CPU sockets/cores.
    -> DEDICATED HOSTS: provides a full physical server, satisfying socket/core-based
       licensing requirements.

Assignment 3 — Design a Basic Two-Tier VPC Architecture

Task: Describe the VPC components needed for a simple, secure web application: a public-facing web server and a private backend database that should never be directly reachable from the internet, but which still needs outbound internet access for software updates.

Solution 
1. Create one VPC (e.g. CIDR 10.0.0.0/16).

2. Create a PUBLIC subnet (e.g. 10.0.1.0/24) for the web server:
   - Attach an Internet Gateway (IGW) to the VPC.
   - Add a route in this subnet's route table: 0.0.0.0/0 -> IGW.
   - Launch the EC2 web server here, with a Security Group allowing inbound
     HTTP/HTTPS (80/443) from the internet.

3. Create a PRIVATE subnet (e.g. 10.0.2.0/24) for the database:
   - Do NOT attach a route to the Internet Gateway (keeps it unreachable from outside).
   - Launch the RDS database here, with a Security Group allowing inbound traffic
     ONLY from the web server's Security Group (not from the internet).

4. Add a NAT Gateway in the public subnet, and add a route in the private
   subnet's route table: 0.0.0.0/0 -> NAT Gateway.
   -> This lets the private database instance reach the internet OUTBOUND
      (e.g. to download OS patches) without ever being reachable INBOUND
      from the internet.

5. (Best practice) Repeat steps 2-3 in a second Availability Zone, and place
   an Elastic Load Balancer in front of the web servers, for high availability.

S3 is designed for 99.999999999% (11 nines) durability. 99.99% is the availability figure for S3 Standard - a commonly confused pair.
Q32. Which S3 storage class offers the LOWEST storage cost, with retrieval taking up to 12 hours?
  • A. S3 Standard-IA
  • B. S3 Glacier Instant Retrieval
  • C. S3 Glacier Deep Archive
  • D. S3 Intelligent-Tiering
Answer: C
S3 Glacier Deep Archive is the lowest-cost class, meant for long-term archival with retrieval up to 12 hours.
Q33. Which storage service can be mounted concurrently by many EC2 instances across multiple Availability Zones at once?
  • A. Amazon EBS
  • B. Amazon EFS
  • C. Instance Store
  • D. Amazon S3 Glacier
Answer: B
EFS is a shared file system mountable by many instances at once across AZs; EBS attaches to only one instance at a time within one AZ.
Q34. Which EC2 pricing option offers up to ~90% savings but can be reclaimed by AWS with only a 2-minute warning?
  • A. Reserved Instances
  • B. Savings Plans
  • C. Spot Instances
  • D. On-Demand Instances
Answer: C
Spot Instances offer the deepest discounts but can be reclaimed with only a 2-minute warning - suitable only for fault-tolerant, flexible workloads.
Q35. Which compute service lets you run code without provisioning or managing any servers, billed per millisecond of execution?
  • A. Amazon EC2
  • B. AWS Lambda
  • C. Amazon Lightsail
  • D. AWS Elastic Beanstalk
Answer: B
Lambda is the serverless compute service billed per millisecond of actual execution time, with no charge when idle.
Q36. What is the PRIMARY purpose of an RDS Multi-AZ deployment?
  • A. To scale out read traffic across multiple copies
  • B. To provide high availability and automatic failover
  • C. To reduce storage costs
  • D. To enable NoSQL queries
Answer: B
Multi-AZ maintains a synchronous standby in a different AZ for high availability/failover - NOT for read scaling (that's what Read Replicas are for).
Q37. A gaming application needs a NoSQL database with single-digit millisecond latency at any scale, with no servers to manage. Which service fits?
  • A. Amazon RDS
  • B. Amazon Redshift
  • C. Amazon DynamoDB
  • D. Amazon Aurora
Answer: C
DynamoDB is the fully managed, serverless NoSQL database built for this exact use case.
Q38. Which service is purpose-built for large-scale data warehousing and business intelligence analytics queries?
  • A. Amazon RDS
  • B. Amazon Redshift
  • C. Amazon DynamoDB
  • D. Amazon ElastiCache
Answer: B
Redshift is the managed data warehouse service optimized for complex analytics queries across massive datasets.
Q39. Which networking component allows instances in a PRIVATE subnet to make outbound internet connections, without allowing inbound connections from the internet?
  • A. Internet Gateway
  • B. NAT Gateway
  • C. Virtual Private Gateway
  • D. Transit Gateway
Answer: B
A NAT Gateway allows outbound-only internet access for private subnet resources.
Q40. Which service provides a DEDICATED, private, physical network connection between an on-premises data center and AWS?
  • A. AWS Site-to-Site VPN
  • B. AWS Direct Connect
  • C. AWS Client VPN
  • D. Amazon CloudFront
Answer: B
Direct Connect establishes a dedicated physical link bypassing the public internet; VPN uses an encrypted connection OVER the public internet.
Q41. Which messaging service should be used to broadcast one message out to many different subscribers simultaneously (fan-out)?
  • A. Amazon SQS
  • B. Amazon SNS
  • C. AWS Step Functions
  • D. Amazon EventBridge
Answer: B
SNS is the pub/sub service designed for fan-out to many subscribers at once; SQS is a queue for one/pooled consumers pulling messages.
Q42. Which service should be used to run and monitor performance metrics/alarms for AWS resources?
  • A. AWS CloudTrail
  • B. Amazon CloudWatch
  • C. AWS Config
  • D. AWS Artifact
Answer: B
CloudWatch handles performance monitoring/metrics/alarms; CloudTrail handles API activity auditing.
Q43. Which service lets developers define entire AWS infrastructure as a version-controlled JSON/YAML template for repeatable deployment?
  • A. AWS Systems Manager
  • B. AWS CloudFormation
  • C. AWS Config
  • D. AWS OpsWorks
Answer: B
CloudFormation is AWS's Infrastructure as Code service using JSON/YAML templates.
Q44. A company needs to run containers without managing any underlying EC2 instances at all. Which should they use?
  • A. Amazon EC2 with Docker installed manually
  • B. AWS Fargate
  • C. Amazon EFS
  • D. AWS Snowball
Answer: B
AWS Fargate is the serverless compute engine for containers, removing the need to provision/manage the underlying instances.

Domain 4: Billing, Pricing, and Support (Questions 45-50)

Q45. A production system is down and the company needs a guaranteed response time under 1 hour. What is the MINIMUM support plan that provides this?
  • A. Basic
  • B. Developer
  • C. Business
  • D. Enterprise On-Ramp
Answer: C
The Business plan guarantees under 1 hour response for a production system down; Developer only offers general guidance response times, with no such guarantee.
Q46. Which support plan is the first to include a dedicated Technical Account Manager (TAM)?
  • A. Business
  • B. Developer
  • C. Enterprise
  • D. Basic
Answer: C
The Enterprise plan is the first to include a dedicated TAM (Enterprise On-Ramp includes a pool of TAMs, not one dedicated TAM).
Q47. Which free tool lets you visualize and forecast AWS cost and usage trends over time?
  • A. AWS Budgets
  • B. AWS Cost Explorer
  • C. AWS Cost and Usage Report
  • D. AWS Pricing Calculator
Answer: B
Cost Explorer is the free tool to visualize, analyze, and forecast AWS cost/usage over time.
Q48. What generally happens to the price of AWS data transfer INTO AWS versus OUT of AWS to the internet?
  • A. Both are always free
  • B. Transfer IN is generally free; transfer OUT is generally charged
  • C. Transfer OUT is generally free; transfer IN is generally charged
  • D. Both are always charged at the same rate
Answer: B
Data transfer into AWS is typically free; data transfer out to the internet is typically charged, and is often an overlooked cost.
Q49. Which AWS Organizations feature provides a single combined bill and volume pricing discounts across multiple member accounts?
  • A. Service Control Policies
  • B. Consolidated Billing
  • C. AWS Budgets
  • D. Cost Explorer
Answer: B
Consolidated Billing combines usage across member accounts for volume discounts and delivers one combined bill.
Q50. Which AWS Free Tier category includes offers that NEVER expire, such as Lambda's first 1 million requests per month?
  • A. 12 Months Free
  • B. Always Free
  • C. Trials
  • D. Enterprise Free Tier
Answer: B
"Always Free" offers never expire and are available to all customers regardless of account age.
