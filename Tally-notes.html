Part A — Getting Started with Tally Prime

1. What is Tally Prime?

Tally Prime is accounting software used by businesses to record their day-to-day financial transactions (sales, purchases, payments, receipts), manage stock/inventory, calculate tax (GST), and automatically generate financial reports like the Balance Sheet and Profit & Loss Account — all without manual calculation.

  • It replaces the traditional paper-based system of maintaining account books (Cash Book, Ledger, Journal) by hand.
  • Once a transaction (a "voucher") is entered once, Tally automatically updates every related report.
  • Used widely by shops, traders, manufacturers, and accounting firms across India.

2. Basics of Accounting – The Golden Rules

Before using Tally, it helps to understand the simple rules that decide whether an amount is Debited (Dr) or Credited (Cr). Every account in Tally belongs to one of three types, each with its own golden rule.

  • Personal Account (accounts of persons, firms, companies, e.g. a customer or supplier): Debit the receiver, Credit the giver.
  • Real Account (assets and properties, e.g. Cash, Furniture, Machinery): Debit what comes in, Credit what goes out.
  • Nominal Account (expenses, incomes, losses, gains, e.g. Rent, Salary, Commission Received): Debit all expenses and losses, Credit all incomes and gains.
Example: Paid Rs.5,000 cash as rent.
- Rent is a Nominal Account (an expense) -> Debit Rent A/c (expense increasing)
- Cash is a Real Account -> Credit Cash A/c (cash going out)
Entry: Rent A/c Dr 5,000
           To Cash A/c   5,000

3. Types of Accounting Vouchers – A Quick Preview

In Tally, every transaction is entered through a "Voucher" – a digital form that matches a specific type of transaction. Each voucher type has its own shortcut key, covered in detail in Part C.

  • Payment – money going out (e.g. paying a supplier).
  • Receipt – money coming in (e.g. from a customer).
  • Contra – transfer between own cash and bank accounts.
  • Journal – non-cash adjustment entries (e.g. depreciation).
  • Sales and Purchase – goods/services sold or bought.

4. The Tally Prime Screen (Gateway of Tally)

When Tally Prime opens (after selecting a company), the main screen is called the Gateway of Tally. Understanding its layout makes navigating the software much easier.

  • Top Menu Bar – company name, current date, and version info.
  • Gateway of Tally (centre) – the main menu listing Masters, Transactions (Vouchers), Utilities, and Reports.
  • Button Bar (right side) – on-screen buttons showing the keyboard shortcut for common actions (most tasks can be done faster using these shortcut keys directly).
  • Calculator Panel (bottom) – a handy on-screen calculator, opened anytime with Ctrl+N.
  • Pressing Esc repeatedly always steps back to the previous screen, eventually returning to the Gateway of Tally.

5. Creating a Company

A "Company" in Tally represents one complete business/organization whose books of accounts are maintained separately. This must be created first before anything else can be done.

  • From the startup screen, select "Create Company".
  • Enter the Company Name, Mailing Address, State, and Financial Year Beginning date.
  • Select the Currency (Base Currency Symbol, e.g. ₹ for Rupees).
  • Press Ctrl+A (or Enter through all fields) to save.

6. Loading, Selecting & Shutting a Company

  • Select Company – if a company is already created but not open, go to Gateway of Tally > press K (Company) > Select Company, or use shortcut F1 from the startup screen.
  • Shut Company – closes the currently open company without closing Tally itself. Use shortcut Alt+F1.
  • Multiple companies can exist in Tally, but only the required one(s) need to be open at a time.

7. F11 (Company Features) & F12 (Configuration)

These two screens control what features are switched on for the company and how Tally behaves – they must be set up correctly before entering GST, inventory, or advanced transactions.

  • F11 – Features – turns major functional areas ON/OFF for this company: Accounting Features, Inventory Features, Statutory & Taxation (GST, TDS), and more.
  • F12 – Configuration – controls how screens/fields display and behave (e.g. showing extra columns, default entry formats). Configuration applies more generally, across all companies.

Part B — Masters (Ledgers, Groups & Stock)

8. What is a Ledger and a Group?

A Ledger is an individual account, such as "Cash", "ABC Traders (Customer)", or "Rent Paid" – every single transaction must involve at least two ledgers. A Group is a category that similar ledgers are organized under, so reports (like the Balance Sheet) can total them together correctly.

  • Tally already provides 28 built-in "Primary Groups" ready to use – you do not need to create these.
  • Important default groups: Capital Account, Loans (Liability), Current Liabilities, Fixed Assets, Current Assets, Sundry Debtors (customers who owe money), Sundry Creditors (suppliers owed money), Sales Accounts, Purchase Accounts, Direct/Indirect Expenses, Direct/Indirect Incomes, Bank Accounts, Cash-in-Hand, Duties & Taxes.
  • Every ledger you create must be placed "Under" one of these groups (or a custom sub-group you create).

9. Creating a Single Ledger

  • Go to Gateway of Tally > Create > Ledger (or shortcut Alt+C from most transaction screens for a quick create).
  • Type the Ledger Name.
  • Select the correct Group "Under" which it belongs.
  • If it is a Cash/Bank ledger or Debtor/Creditor, enter the Opening Balance if any exists on the start date.
  • Press Ctrl+A to save.

10. Creating Multiple Ledgers Together

Instead of creating ledgers one at a time, Tally allows creating several ledgers in a single grid-style screen, which saves time when setting up a new company.

  • Go to Gateway of Tally > Create (under Multiple Ledgers) > Multiple Ledgers.
  • Select the Group under which all these ledgers should be created (or choose "All Items" to pick a different group per row).
  • Type each ledger name and its opening balance directly into the grid.

11. Altering & Deleting Ledgers

  • Alter – Gateway of Tally > Alter > Ledger > select the ledger > change details > Ctrl+A to save.
  • Delete – open the ledger in Alter mode, then press Alt+D. A ledger with existing transactions cannot be deleted until those entries are removed.

12. Stock Groups & Stock Categories

Just as Groups organize ledgers, Stock Groups organize similar stock items together (e.g. "Electronics", "Stationery"). Stock Categories provide a second, independent way to classify items (e.g. by brand), useful for cross-analysis reports. Both are optional but keep large inventories organized.

  • Gateway of Tally > Create > Stock Group – enter a name, choose whether it should have sub-groups.
  • Gateway of Tally > Create > Stock Category – enter a name similarly.

13. Units of Measure

A Unit of Measure defines how a stock item is counted or measured (e.g. Nos, Kg, Litre, Box) – every stock item must be assigned a unit before it can be created.

  • Gateway of Tally > Create > Unit.
  • Choose Simple (a single unit, e.g. Nos, Kg) or Compound (a unit made of two units, e.g. 1 Box = 12 Nos).
  • Type the Symbol (e.g. "Kg") and the Formal Name (e.g. "Kilograms").
Gateway of Tally -> Create -> Unit
Type: Simple
Symbol      : Nos
Formal Name : Numbers
Number of Decimal Places: 0
Press Ctrl+A to Save

14. Creating a Stock Item

A Stock Item is the actual product/goods that a business buys and sells – e.g. "LED Bulb 9W". Stock Items are only needed if the company deals in physical goods; a pure services business may not need this at all.

  • Gateway of Tally > Create > Stock Item.
  • Enter the Name, select the Stock Group (or "Not Applicable"), and select the Unit of measure.
  • Optionally set GST Applicability and tax rate directly on the item.
  • Enter Opening Balance (quantity and value) if existing stock is being entered.
Gateway of Tally -> Create -> Stock Item

Press Ctrl+A to Save

15. Godowns / Locations

A Godown represents a physical storage location (e.g. Main Warehouse, Branch Store) – useful when a business stores stock in more than one place and needs to track quantities location-wise.

  • Gateway of Tally > Create > Godown.
  • By default, Tally provides one Godown called "Main Location"; additional godowns can be added as needed.

16. Voucher Types (Overview)

A Voucher Type is the template/category a transaction is recorded under (Payment, Receipt, Sales, Purchase, etc.). Tally provides all standard voucher types by default; custom voucher types (e.g. a separate "Cash Sales" type) can also be created for specific business needs.

  • Gateway of Tally > Create > Voucher Type (only needed for custom types).
  • The full list of standard voucher types and their shortcut keys is covered in Part C.

Part C — Accounting Vouchers

17. Voucher Entry Basics

All transactions are entered from Gateway of Tally > Vouchers (or by pressing V). Each type of transaction opens on a different function key, listed below.

  • Press F2 inside any voucher screen to change the voucher date.
  • Type the Ledger name and Tally will auto-suggest matches as you type (no need to type the full name).
  • Press Ctrl+A to save the voucher once entry is complete (or Enter through every field to the end).
  • Press Page Up to view the previous voucher, Page Down for the next.
  • Press Alt+D while viewing a saved voucher to delete it.

18. Payment Voucher (F5)

Records money going OUT of the business – for example, paying a supplier, paying rent, or paying salaries, whether by cash or bank.

  • Gateway of Tally > Vouchers > press F5.
  • Select the ledger being paid FROM (Cash or Bank) in the "Account" field.
  • Select the ledger being paid TO (e.g. an expense or a supplier) and enter the amount.
  • Add a Narration (short description) if needed, then Ctrl+A to save.
Gateway of Tally -> Vouchers -> F5 (Payment)
Account (paid from) : Cash
Particulars (paid to): Rent Paid       Amount: 5,000
Narration: Being rent paid for the month
Press Ctrl+A to Save

19. Receipt Voucher (F6)

Records money coming IN to the business – for example, receiving payment from a customer, or receiving a loan.

  • Gateway of Tally > Vouchers > press F6.
  • Select the ledger receiving the money INTO (Cash or Bank) in the "Account" field.
  • Select the ledger the money is coming FROM (e.g. a customer) and enter the amount.
Gateway of Tally -> Vouchers -> F6 (Receipt)
Account (received into) : HDFC Bank
Particulars (received from): Ramesh Traders   Amount: 10,000
Narration: Being payment received against outstanding bill
Press Ctrl+A to Save

20. Contra Voucher (F4)

Records a transfer of funds between the business's OWN cash and bank accounts only – for example, depositing cash into the bank, or withdrawing cash from the bank. Never used for transactions with outside parties.

  • Gateway of Tally > Vouchers > press F4.
  • Both the "Account" and "Particulars" must be Cash/Bank ledgers only.
Gateway of Tally -> Vouchers -> F4 (Contra)
Account (deposited into) : HDFC Bank
Particulars (from)        : Cash          Amount: 20,000
Narration: Being cash deposited into bank
Press Ctrl+A to Save

21. Journal Voucher (F7)

Records non-cash adjustment entries that don't directly involve cash or bank – for example, depreciation, writing off a bad debt, or correcting an entry. This is where the debit/credit golden rules are applied most directly.

  • Gateway of Tally > Vouchers > press F7.
  • Select the ledger to Debit and enter the amount.
  • Select the ledger to Credit – Tally auto-fills the same amount to keep the entry balanced.
Gateway of Tally -> Vouchers -> F7 (Journal)

Narration: Being depreciation charged on furniture
Press Ctrl+A to Save

22. Sales Voucher (F8)

Records goods or services SOLD to a customer – the most frequently used voucher in a trading business, and where GST is calculated automatically if enabled.

  • Gateway of Tally > Vouchers > press F8.
  • Select the Party (customer) ledger.
  • Select "Item Invoice" mode (if inventory is used) and enter each Stock Item, quantity, and rate; Tally calculates the total automatically.
  • Select the GST tax ledgers (CGST/SGST or IGST) so tax is added automatically.

  • Gateway of Tally > Vouchers > press F9.

  • Select the GST tax ledgers to add input tax automatically.

24. Debit Note & Credit Note

These handle RETURNS after a Sales or Purchase has already been recorded. A Debit Note (Alt+F5) is used when the business returns goods to a supplier (a purchase return) – it reduces what is owed to the supplier. A Credit Note (Alt+F6) is used when a customer returns goods (a sales return) – it reduces what the customer owes.

  • Both must first be enabled in F11 Features ("Use Debit/Credit Notes? Yes").
  • Entry style is identical to Purchase/Sales vouchers, just recorded as a return.
Gateway of Tally -> Vouchers -> Alt+F6 (Credit Note)
Party A/c Name: Ramesh Traders
Name of Item        Qty   Rate    Amount
LED Bulb 9W           2    80      160
Narration: Being 2 defective bulbs returned by customer
Press Ctrl+A to Save

25. Sales Order & Purchase Order

Orders record an AGREEMENT to sell/buy in the future – they do not affect the accounts or stock balances until they are actually converted into a Sales/Purchase voucher later. Useful for tracking pending orders.

  • Sales Order – shortcut Alt+F5 (must be enabled in F11).
  • Purchase Order – shortcut Alt+F4 (must be enabled in F11).
  • When the goods are actually delivered/received, the order can be recalled and converted directly into a Sales or Purchase voucher.

Part D — Inventory Vouchers

26. Stock Journal

Used to record the movement of stock WITHOUT any purchase or sale involved – for example, converting raw material into a finished product, or transferring stock between godowns.

  • Gateway of Tally > Vouchers > press Alt+F7.
  • Enter the item(s) "Source" (what is consumed) and "Destination" (what is produced/received).

27. Physical Stock Voucher

Used to record the ACTUAL quantity of stock counted physically in the godown, when it differs from what Tally's book records show (due to damage, theft, or counting errors). This does not change financial value directly, only quantity records for verification.

  • Gateway of Tally > Vouchers > press Ctrl+F7.
  • Select the item and enter the actual physically counted quantity.

28. Delivery Note & Receipt Note

These record the physical MOVEMENT of goods, separately from the financial Sales/Purchase entry – useful for tracking dispatch/receipt before an invoice is raised.

  • Delivery Note (Alt+F8) – records goods physically dispatched to a customer.
  • Receipt Note (Alt+F9) – records goods physically received from a supplier.
  • Both can later be linked to the actual Sales/Purchase voucher when it is raised.

29. Godown Transfer

Moves stock quantity from one godown/location to another within the same business, without changing ownership or value – recorded through the Stock Journal by selecting a different Source and Destination godown for the same item.

Part E — GST (Goods and Services Tax)

30. What is GST? – Quick Basics

GST is a single indirect tax charged on the sale of goods and services in India, replacing earlier separate taxes like VAT and Service Tax. Tally Prime can calculate GST automatically on every sale/purchase once it is set up correctly.

  • CGST (Central GST) and SGST (State GST) – charged together, split equally, on sales WITHIN the same state.
  • IGST (Integrated GST) – charged as one single tax on sales BETWEEN different states.
  • GST rate slabs commonly used: 0%, 5%, 12%, 18%, 28%.

31. Enabling GST in Tally Prime

  • Go to Gateway of Tally > press F11 (Company Features).
  • Set "Enable Goods and Services Tax (GST)" to Yes.
  • Enter the company's GSTIN (GST registration number) and the State (this determines whether a sale is Intra-state, using CGST+SGST, or Inter-state, using IGST).
Gateway of Tally -> F11 -> Statutory & Taxation
Enable Goods and Services Tax (GST)? Yes
State                                : Telangana
GSTIN/UIN                           : 36ABCDE1234F1Z5
Press Ctrl+A to Save

32. Creating GST Tax Ledgers

Separate ledgers must be created for each type of GST so tax amounts are shown and tracked correctly on invoices and in returns.

  • Gateway of Tally > Create > Ledger.
  • Group each under "Duties & Taxes"; set Type of Duty/Tax as "GST".
  • Create separate ledgers for CGST, SGST, and IGST.
Gateway of Tally -> Create -> Ledger
Name  : CGST
Under : Duties & Taxes
Type of Duty/Tax: GST     Tax Type: Central Tax
Press Ctrl+A to Save
(repeat similarly for SGST -> State Tax, and IGST -> Integrated Tax)

33. Recording a GST Sales Invoice

Once GST is enabled and the tax ledgers exist, GST sales entry uses the normal Sales voucher (F8) – Tally calculates the tax automatically based on the item's GST rate.

  • Enter the Sales voucher as usual (Party, Stock Item, Quantity, Rate).
  • After the item line(s), select the CGST and SGST ledgers (for an intra-state sale) – Tally auto-calculates the tax amount based on the item's configured GST rate.

35. GST Reports – GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B

Tally automatically compiles GST return reports from the vouchers already entered, ready to be filed on the government GST portal.

  • GSTR-1 – a report of all outward supplies (sales) made during the period. Path: Gateway of Tally > Display More Reports > Statutory Reports > GST Reports > GSTR-1.
  • GSTR-3B – a monthly summary return showing total tax liability and input tax credit. Same path, select GSTR-3B.
  • Tally highlights any entries with missing GST details in red/exception, so they can be corrected before filing.

Part F — Financial Reports

36. Trial Balance

A report listing every ledger with its Debit or Credit closing balance side by side – used to check that total Debits equal total Credits before preparing final accounts. If the two totals don't match, an entry error exists somewhere.

  • Path: Gateway of Tally > Display More Reports > Trial Balance.
  • Press Alt+F1 for a detailed (group-wise expanded) view.

37. Profit & Loss Account

Shows whether the business made a profit or loss over a period, by comparing total Income against total Expenses. All Direct/Indirect Income and Direct/Indirect Expense ledgers automatically flow into this report.

  • Path: Gateway of Tally > Display More Reports > Profit & Loss A/c.
  • Press Alt+F2 to change the reporting period/date range.

38. Balance Sheet

Shows the financial POSITION of the business on a given date – what it OWNS (Assets) versus what it OWES (Liabilities and Capital). Assets must always equal Liabilities + Capital.

  • Path: Gateway of Tally > Display More Reports > Balance Sheet.
  • The current year's Profit/Loss (from Section 37) automatically appears here under Capital.

39. Day Book

A chronological (date-wise) list of every single voucher entered on a given day (or date range) – the fastest way to review or verify all transactions recorded.

  • Path: Gateway of Tally > Display More Reports > Day Book, or shortcut Alt+G (Go To) > type "Day Book".
  • Press F2 to change the date, or Alt+F2 to view a date range.

40. Ledger Report (Ledger Voucher Display)

Shows every transaction posted to ONE specific ledger, along with its running balance – equivalent to a traditional handwritten ledger account page.

  • Path: Gateway of Tally > Display More Reports > Account Books > Ledger > select the ledger name.

41. Stock Summary

Shows the quantity and value of every stock item currently held, grouped by Stock Group – the inventory equivalent of the Balance Sheet.

  • Path: Gateway of Tally > Display More Reports > Stock Summary.
  • Drill down (press Enter) on any item to see its full movement (purchases in, sales out).

42. Cash Book & Bank Book

Specialized Ledger reports (see Section 40) shown specifically for the Cash-in-Hand and Bank ledgers, displaying all receipts and payments through them with a running balance.

  • Path: Gateway of Tally > Display More Reports > Account Books > Cash/Bank Book.

43. Ratio Analysis

Automatically calculates key financial health indicators from the accounting data already entered – e.g. Current Ratio (ability to pay short-term debts) and Gross Profit %, without needing manual calculation.

  • Path: Gateway of Tally > Display More Reports > Ratio Analysis.

Part G — Banking & Other Features

44. Bank Reconciliation Statement (BRS)

Matches the bank balance as per Tally's books against the balance shown on the actual bank statement, marking off which cheques/transactions have been cleared by the bank and which are still pending – used to catch missing or duplicate entries.

  • Open the Bank ledger report, then press F5 to switch to Reconciliation mode.
  • Enter the actual "Bank Date" (the date it cleared per the bank statement) next to each transaction.

45. Cheque Printing

Allows a cheque to be printed directly from Tally when making a Payment voucher through a bank ledger, avoiding manual handwriting of cheques.

  • Must first configure the Bank ledger with its Cheque Book details (cheque number range) in the ledger master.
  • While saving a Payment voucher through that bank, choose "Yes" when prompted to print a cheque.

46. Backup and Restore Company Data

Backup creates a safety copy of company data (protecting against data loss); Restore brings that backup data back into Tally if needed (e.g. on a new computer).

  • Backup: Gateway of Tally > press Ctrl+Alt+B (or Alt+K > Backup) > select the company > choose the destination folder.
  • Restore: Alt+K > Restore > select the backup source folder > choose destination.
  • It is good practice to take a backup at the end of every working day.

47. Security Control (Users & Passwords)

Restricts who can access a company's data and what they are allowed to do – important when multiple staff use the same Tally installation.

  • Gateway of Tally > press F11 > set "Enable Security Control?" to Yes.
  • Create user types (e.g. Owner, Data Entry) with different levels of permission, and assign a username/password to each.

Part H — Payroll

48. Enabling Payroll

Payroll is Tally Prime's module for calculating and recording employee salaries, deductions, and net pay. It must be switched on before any employee or salary data can be entered, just like GST or Inventory.

Payroll
  • Go to F11 (Company Features) and set "Maintain Payroll? Yes" and "Maintain more than one Payroll / Cost Category? No" (for a single, simple payroll setup).
  • Once enabled, new menu options appear: Employee, Employee Group, Pay Head, Attendance/Production Type, and the Payroll Voucher.
  • Payroll works together with normal accounting – every salary payment still ultimately reduces the Bank/Cash ledger, just calculated automatically from the employee's pay structure.
Path: Gateway of Tally -> F11 (Company Features)

Maintain Payroll?                        : Yes
Maintain more than one Payroll/Cost Category?: No
Press Ctrl+A to save.

49. Creating Employees & Employee Groups

An Employee Group is a category used to organise staff (e.g. by department or branch), just like a ledger Group organises ledgers. An Employee master stores one staff member's personal and employment details.

Payroll
  • (Optional) Create an Employee Group first, e.g. "Office Staff" or "Factory Workers", if you want to organise employees into departments.
  • Go to Create > Employee. Fill in Name, Employee Group (if used), Date of Joining, Gender, and Designation.
  • Additional details (PAN, bank account for salary transfer, address) can be filled under "Employee Details" for statutory accuracy, though a beginner can leave these blank for practice.
Path: Gateway of Tally -> Create -> Employee

Under (Employee Group): Office Staff

Function         : Accounts

50. Pay Heads (Earnings & Deductions)

A Pay Head is one component of a salary structure – for example "Basic Pay" (an Earning) or "Provident Fund" (a Deduction). Each Pay Head defines whether it adds to or subtracts from the employee's pay, and how its amount is calculated (a fixed value, a percentage of another pay head, or based on attendance).

Payroll
  • Earnings for Employees – e.g. Basic Pay (flat amount), HRA (often a % of Basic Pay), Conveyance Allowance.
  • Deductions from Employees – e.g. Provident Fund (often 12% of Basic Pay), Professional Tax, Loan Recovery.
  • Go to Create > Pay Head, give it a Name, choose its Pay Head Type (Earnings for Employees / Deductions from Employees), select the Income Type, and set the Calculation Type (Flat Rate, or "On Specified Formula" to calculate as a % of another pay head such as Basic Pay).
Path: Gateway of Tally -> Create -> Pay Head

Name            : Basic Pay
Pay Head Type   : Earnings for Employees
Under           : Indirect Expenses
Calculation Type: Flat Rate

Name            : HRA
Pay Head Type   : Earnings for Employees
Calculation Type: On Specified Formula
Formula         : 40% of Basic Pay

Name            : Provident Fund
Pay Head Type   : Deductions from Employees
Calculation Type: On Specified Formula
Formula         : 12% of Basic Pay

Press Ctrl+A to save each Pay Head.

51. Salary Details & Attendance/Production Types

Salary Details link an employee to the specific Pay Heads that make up their pay structure, with the actual amount for each. Attendance/Production Type records days present, days absent, or units produced, which some Pay Heads (like an attendance-based Basic Pay) use in their calculation.

Payroll
  • Go to Gateway of Tally > Create > Salary Details, select the employee, then add each applicable Pay Head with its amount.
  • For attendance-based pay, go to Create > Attendance/Production Type (e.g. "Present", "Absent", or "Paid Leave") and record daily/monthly attendance through the Attendance Voucher before running payroll.
Path: Gateway of Tally -> Create -> Salary Details -> Suresh Kumar

Pay Head        Amount
Basic Pay        20,000
HRA               8,000  (auto = 40% of Basic Pay)
Provident Fund    2,400  (auto = 12% of Basic Pay, deduction)

Press Ctrl+A to save.

52. Processing Payroll & Generating Payslips

Once employees and their salary structures are set up, the Payroll Voucher is used each pay period to actually calculate and record that month's salary, which Tally can then print or export as a Payslip.

Payroll
  • Press Ctrl+F4 (or go to Vouchers > Payroll Voucher) to open a new Payroll Voucher.
  • Select the employee (or "Auto Fill" to process all employees together for the month), and Tally calculates each Pay Head automatically from the Salary Details set up earlier.
  • Save the voucher; the payroll expense is now recorded exactly like any other accounting entry, reducing Bank/Cash when actually paid.
  • View the printable Payslip from Gateway of Tally > Display More Reports > Payroll Reports > Payslip.
Path: Gateway of Tally -> Vouchers -> Ctrl+F4 (Payroll Voucher)

Employee           : Suresh Kumar
Payroll Ledger     : Salary Payable (Under: Current Liabilities)
Pay Period         : April 2025

Basic Pay   : 20,000  (Earning)
HRA         :  8,000  (Earning)
Provident Fund: 2,400 (Deduction)
Net Pay     : 25,600

Press Ctrl+A to save. Then:
Gateway of Tally -> Display More Reports -> Payroll Reports -> Payslip
-> select Suresh Kumar -> Print (Alt+P)

Part I — Advanced Features

53. Cost Centres & Cost Categories

A Cost Centre lets you track income or expense against a specific department, project, or branch (e.g. "Marketing", "Hyderabad Branch"), separate from the ledger it is posted to – useful for seeing which department is spending or earning what, on top of the normal ledger-wise view. Cost Categories group multiple, parallel sets of cost centres (e.g. tracking both "Department" and "Project" at the same time).

  • Enable in F11: "Maintain Cost Centres? Yes".
  • Create Cost Centres (e.g. "Marketing", "Production") under Gateway of Tally > Create > Cost Centre.
  • When entering a voucher (e.g. a Payment for an expense), Tally will now prompt for the Cost Centre to allocate that amount against, in addition to the ledger.
  • View the result in Display More Reports > Statements of Accounts > Cost Centres, which shows totals per cost centre.
Path: Gateway of Tally -> F11 -> Maintain Cost Centres? Yes -> Ctrl+A

Path: Gateway of Tally -> Create -> Cost Centre
Name: Marketing   Under: Primary

Path: Gateway of Tally -> Vouchers -> F5 (Payment)
Account: Cash   Particulars: Advertisement Expense   Amount: 5,000
Cost Centre: Marketing   Amount: 5,000   (Tally asks for this automatically)

54. Budgets & Scenarios

A Budget lets you set a planned spending/income limit for a ledger or group over a period, so Tally can show actual figures against the budgeted figures and highlight variances. A Scenario allows "what-if" reporting using provisional (non-final) vouchers that do not affect the real books until confirmed.

  • Go to Gateway of Tally > Create > Budget, give it a name, select the period, and set budgeted amounts against chosen Groups/Ledgers.
  • Compare actual vs budget using Display More Reports > Statements of Accounts > Budget Variance.
Path: Gateway of Tally -> Create -> Budget

Name       : Annual Budget 2025-26
Period     : 1-Apr-2025 to 31-Mar-2026
Ledger     : Advertisement Expense   Budgeted Amount: 60,000

Path: Display More Reports -> Statements of Accounts -> Budget Variance
(shows actual spend against the 60,000 budgeted, and the variance)

55. Multi-Currency

Multi-Currency allows a business to record transactions in a foreign currency (e.g. USD) while Tally automatically converts and reports them in the base currency (e.g. INR) using an exchange rate, essential for import/export businesses.

  • Enable in F11: "Enable Multi-Currency? Yes".
  • Create additional Currency masters (Gateway of Tally > Create > Currency) for each foreign currency needed, e.g. USD.
  • Set the exchange rate for that currency on the date of the transaction.
  • When creating a ledger for a foreign party/bank, select the relevant currency; vouchers in that currency then auto-convert to base currency value for reports.
Path: Gateway of Tally -> F11 -> Enable Multi-Currency? Yes -> Ctrl+A

Path: Gateway of Tally -> Create -> Currency
Symbol: $   Formal Name: US Dollars
Exchange Rate (as on date): 1 USD = 83.00 INR

56. Data Export, Import & Voucher Printing

Tally Prime can send reports/vouchers out to other formats for sharing, and print professional invoices directly from a voucher screen.

  • Export – from any report or voucher, press Alt+E, choose a format (Excel, PDF, XML, JSON), and a save location.
  • Import – used to bring master or voucher data in from another Tally company or an XML/JSON file, via Gateway of Tally > Import Data.
  • Print – press Alt+P from any open voucher or report to print it (e.g. printing a Sales Invoice to hand to a customer, or a Payslip for an employee).
From an open Sales Voucher:
Press Alt+P -> Print -> select printer -> Print

From the Trial Balance report:
Press Alt+E -> Export -> Format: PDF -> Ctrl+A to confirm and save

(1) Started business with Rs.50,000 cash.
(2) Purchased furniture for Rs.10,000 cash.
(3) Purchased goods worth Rs.15,000 on credit from "Vendor A".
(4) Sold goods worth Rs.8,000 for cash.
(5) Paid Rs.2,000 cash as electricity bill.
(6) Received Rs.6,000 cash from a debtor "Customer B" (create with an opening balance of Rs.6,000 Dr).

Solution

2. Create Ledgers:
   Capital Account   Under: Capital Account
   Furniture         Under: Fixed Assets

   Account: Cash   Particulars: Capital Account   Amount: 50,000

4. Transaction 2 - Payment (F5):
   Account: Cash   Particulars: Furniture   Amount: 10,000

5. Transaction 3 - Purchase (F9), Accounting Invoice mode (no stock item needed):
   Party A/c Name: Vendor A
   Particulars: Purchase Accounts   Amount: 15,000

6. Transaction 4 - Sales (F8), Accounting Invoice mode:
   Party A/c Name: Cash (or Cash Sales)
   Particulars: Sales Accounts   Amount: 8,000

   Account: Cash   Particulars: Electricity Charges   Amount: 2,000

   Account: Cash   Particulars: Customer B   Amount: 6,000

   Confirm Cash balance = 50,000 - 10,000 + 8,000 - 2,000 + 6,000 = 52,000
